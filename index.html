CrashGen: Learned Red-Teaming for Grasp Robustness Under Geometric Variation

Anonymous Submission

CrashGen-guided blue-teaming. Standard fine-tuning on the original training objects can leave geometric failure modes unresolved. CrashGen generates diverse CrashShapes from the training objects and uses them for blue-teaming. Fine-tuning on these generated variants improves real-world grasp behavior on held-out objects under geometric variation.

Abstract

Data-driven robotic grasping policies often transfer poorly to held-out objects from a target object category. Fine-tuning on a small set of original training meshes can improve performance near those shapes, but cannot span the geometric variants that cause failures at test time. A direct way to obtain such variants is geometric red-teaming, which searches over constrained shape deformations to find geometries on which a frozen manipulation policy fails, but this simulator-in-the-loop search takes hours per object and cannot be used at training-data scale.

We introduce CrashGen, a point-cloud-conditioned diffusion model that amortizes this search by generating failure-inducing deformation controls in seconds; a Jacobian-field operator maps each control to a physically plausible deformation of the original mesh. Trained once on failure-inducing controls discovered by running this search offline, CrashGen generates such variants for over 80% of held-out objects under simulation validation.

Fine-tuning an off-the-shelf grasp policy (Contact-GraspNet) on a mix of original meshes and CrashGen variants improves grasp success on held-out objects by up to 21% in simulation and 30% on a real robot, relative to a baseline trained only on the original meshes. Together, these results show that amortized geometric red-teaming turns an expensive failure-discovery procedure into scalable training data for robustifying grasp policies against geometric variation.

System Overview

Generating CrashShapes. CrashGen diffuses over handle-point displacements rather than meshes. The model conditions on the original point cloud and handle mask, then predicts sparse displacement controls over the handle-supported subspace. A deterministic Jacobian-field solver maps each sampled control field to an admissible mesh deformation, yielding K CrashShapes.
